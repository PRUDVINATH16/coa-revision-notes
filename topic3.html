📝 Bus Timing (Synchronous vs. Asynchronous) Topic 3

The 1-Sentence Story: Bus timing dictates how masters and slaves coordinate data transfers, either by using a strict common clock (Synchronous) for speed, or a flexible back-and-forth handshake (Asynchronous) to accommodate varying device speeds and distances.

📌 Definitions & Key Terms

Synchronous Bus
A bus that uses a common clock signal; requests and responses occur only at specific clock edges.
Asynchronous Bus
A bus that uses a handshake protocol (Master-ready / Slave-ready) instead of a clock to coordinate transfers.
Handshake
A back-and-forth signaling protocol where the master signals it is ready, and the slave signals it has completed the operation.
Clock Skew
A problem in synchronous buses where the clock signal arrives at different components at slightly different times due to propagation delay over long wires.

⏱️ Synchronous Bus (Key Points)

🔹 Operation & Limitations

  • Operation: Master places address/command at t₀, slave places data at t₁, master strobes data at t₂.
  • Single-Cycle Limitation: Transfer must finish in one clock cycle. The clock period must accommodate the slowest device + propagation delay + setup time.
⏳ Tmin = delay + slowest device response + propagation + margin clock period
  • Major Problems: Clock skew, forces all devices to run at the speed of the slowest device, no error detection (master assumes data is ready).
  • Solution: Multiple-cycle transfers (using a high-frequency clock and a Slave-ready control signal to stretch the transfer across cycles).
  • Use Case: Processor-Memory Bus (short, fast, predictable).

🔄 Asynchronous Bus (Key Points)

🔹 Handshake Protocol & Sequence

  • Control Lines: Master-ready (address/command is valid) and Slave-ready (data/operation is ready).
  • Operation Sequence: Master places address/command → asserts Master-ready → Slave places data → asserts Slave-ready → Master strobes data and removes signals → Slave removes data/signals.
  • Advantages: Automatically accommodates varying delays and speeds; eliminates clock skew; highly reliable.
  • Disadvantages: Slower than synchronous due to "two round-trip delays" (handshake overhead).
  • Use Case: I/O Bus (longer, variable speeds, connects diverse peripherals).

🔒 GOLDEN RULE

Timing coordination ensures every transfer is reliable — synchronous for speed and predictability, asynchronous for flexibility and distance.
